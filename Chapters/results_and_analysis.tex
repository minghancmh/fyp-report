\chapter{Results and Analysis}

In this chapter, we will do a comparison between deploying a Kubernetes workload via the traditional approach and via \texttt{cloudkube}. While \texttt{cloudkube} was initially developed
to meet the needs of the ASR application, this FYP has met the stretch goal of making \texttt{cloudkube} extensible to different types of Kubernetes workloads. Consequently, the subsequent sections
will show the testing and deployment of the ASR application as well as a multi-service, fullstack application to infrastructure provisioned by \texttt{cloudkube}, 

\section{Deployment Timeline}
This subsection will compare the deployment timeline using \texttt{cloudkube} vs the traditioanal manual approach, highlighting the time savings and operational benefits. 
The estimated times presented here are based on my experience, as well as anecdotal, unstructured evidence with other students working on deployment of SpeechLab applications.
As such, they serve as a general guide rather than absolute values, as actual deployment durations vary depending on specific infrastructure configurations, as well as expertise
in cloud technologies. 

\subsection{Traditional Method}

In the context of this subsection, the traditional method of deploying a Kubernetes workload to the cloud refers to using the AWS CLI or AWS Console for manual setup and management.
Due to time and resource constraints, this FYP is focused solely on AWS Cloud, and all deployment/learning times peresented here are based on my personal experience with AWS, and may vary
depending on individual familiarity with the platform and cloud specific technologies.

\subsection{\texttt{cloudkube} Method}

As we can see from Table \ref{table:k8s-cluster-cloudkube}, the learning curve with \texttt{cloudkube} is made more gentle, and execution times are drastically reduced as compared to the 
traditional method (Table \ref{table:k8s-cluster-setup}). Furthermore, the bulk of the time taken as presented in Table \ref{table:k8s-cluster-cloudkube} is for the installation of prerequisites.
Once a developer has set up his/her local environment with \texttt{cloudkube} and its corresponding dependencies, it takes \textbf{no more than 20 minutes} to get a fully
functional Kubernetes cluster in the cloud.  The full workflow with \texttt{cloudkube} can be found in Appendix \ref{appendix:cloudkube-readme}.
